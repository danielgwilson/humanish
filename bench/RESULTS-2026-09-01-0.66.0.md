# Taskly detection benchmark, second run

**2026-09-01, humanish 0.66.0, `openai-computer-use` on hosted E2B desktops, N=3 per arm.** Same
labs, same persona, same mission as [the first run on 0.63.0](RESULTS-2026-09-01.md). Six live
runs, $1.29 total.

Run 1 missed D1 and reported the opposite: "**Clear completed** removed the finished task." The trace shows the participant deleting tasks individually in the same tidy-up pass; the
most likely reading is that it attributed a `Delete` to `Clear completed`. Runs 2 and 3 both saw
the defect ("**Clear completed** did nothing, including when I tried it from the All view. I had
to delete the completed task using its individual **Delete** button instead").

Cumulative over both benchmark runs: 29 of 30. The first run's 15 of 15 was the upper bound it was
labelled as. One miss in six runs is the number to carry, and the miss came with a false claim of
working behaviour, which is the failure mode a reader should expect from this instrument: not an
invented defect, a missed one reported as fine.

## Precision: no invented defects in 3 clean runs

No clean run reported any planted defect class. Every clean run described the filters, Save, and
Clear completed working. What they raised was true of the app as written, the same three findings
as the first benchmark: `Clear completed` stays enabled with nothing completed (3 of 3), no undo or
confirmation on Delete (2 of 3), the `left` count is global rather than per filter (1 of 3).

## What the benchmark found in humanish this time

**Three of six lanes were refused as "not a credible pass" on sentences that were not blockers.**
Two planted runs ("so I could not read its full description", "so I could not tell whether the
rename had actually been saved") and one clean run ("I encountered no blockers or unclear error
output"). Every participant had reached the goal. Two of the three sentences were the participant
describing the planted truncation defect precisely; the third was a participant saying that
nothing blocked it. The same scan refused both keyless local-agent runs on drawDB the same hour
("can't even read the whole thing", "can't tell from the screen").

That is 5 of 5 completed live runs across two labs refused on perception verbs after "can't", plus
one negated clause. Fixed in #565: the verdict scan strips `can't / could not / unable to` when a
perception verb follows, and a clause that begins "encountered no" is a negation as a whole. The
friction scan is unchanged, so those sentences still count as friction and still become feedback
candidates. The bisect of all six transcripts passes on the fixed scan.

Because #476 shipped in the same release, those three bundles read `verdict: blocked`,
`0/1 reached the goal` instead of the old `verdict: pass` next to a refused lane. The bundle and
the lane now agree; on these runs they agreed on the wrong answer. The scan was the weak link
both times, which is the argument for the participant classifying its own outcome in the
schema-constrained local-agent reply rather than a regex inferring it.

## Intervals, because a validity audit of ten agentic benchmarks found none reported them

Recall 29 of 30 across both runs: Wilson 95% interval 0.83 to 0.99. Precision, planted-class
false reports on the clean arm, 0 of 6 runs: the rule-of-three bound says the true rate could be as
high as 0.39 per run and still show zero in six. Both are why the arms stay small honest numbers
rather than a headline percentage. A do-nothing baseline (a participant that takes no action)
reports nothing by construction and is enforced by the harness's engagement guard rather than run
as a control.
